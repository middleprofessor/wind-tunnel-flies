Long term selection on mean-apparent flying speed in Drosophila melanogaster resulted in large increase in body size but trivial changes in wing size and shape

Author

Jeffrey Walker

Published

October 22, 2026

Abstract

Long term selection on mean-apparent flying speed in Drosophila melanogaster resulted in large gains (30-40%) in upwind burst speed, large increases in body weight (29-50%), thorax (15%) and head (12-15%) size, but only very small changes (0-3%) in wing size and shape, including smaller wing area (2.1-3.4%) in females, and no apparent change in wing beat frequency

Following an initial, rapid increase, selected lines have continued to increase mean-apparent-flying speed by 14.7 cm/s per 100 generations for over 600 generations

Selection started on or before 1991 and continued until 2023.

Concerns:

The AA1 and AA2 lines have remarkably similar fit, which could result from

  1. Climate (temperature/humidity) fluctuations in the room during selection runs
  2. Climate (temperature/humidity/light) fluctions in the rearing room. Since 2001 the temperature and humidity have been well controlled.
  3. Husbandry modifications
  4. Gene flow between lines

Selected lines have 30-40% (2.8 – 3.3 SD) higher upwind flight ability than Control lines in generation 384

Method summary:

  1. Compared selected (AA), control (CN) and wild-type lines (K45, N60) with more recent collection from wild than CN lines. 2.individual flies introduced into standard wind tunnel and tested using increasing velocity test (as in fish)
  2. flies stimulated by rod with insect pin that could be poked at fly from behind screen
  3. flights >= 5 cm were considered successful and the wind speed was increased.
  4. Individual flies tested at multiple speeds, until failed to fly >= 5 cm.

Body Weight is 29% (2.3 SD) larger in Selected females and 32% (1.5 SD) larger in Selected males relative to Controls in Generation 384 (Burst flight test)

Body Weight is 38% (3.7 SD) larger in Selected females and 51% (4.9 SD) larger in Selected males relative to Wild Type flies in Generation 615 (wing metrics analysis)

Thorax and head widths are 12-15% (7 SD) larger in Selected lines compared to Wild Type lines

Wing size and shape in Selected flies have only very small differences compared to Wild Type flies, at least relative to flight performance and body size traits

Compared to WT flies, the wings of Selected females are slightly shorter while the wings of Selected males have slightly more distal area

Compared to WT flies, Selected females have 2.1 - 3.4% (.64 - .86 SD) smaller wing area but .7 - 1.5% (.66 - 1.5 SD) greater normalized second moment of area

Compared to WT flies, Selected males have 0.3 - 1.6% (.08 - .4 SD) greater wing area and 0.5 - 1.2% (.22 - .5 SD) greater normalized second moment of area

Compared to WT flies, Selected females have 41% and Selected males have 49% higher wing loading

Relative to 30 species of Drosophilid, wing area in Selected lines is extremely small and wing loading is exremely large
